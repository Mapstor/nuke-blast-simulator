About this scenarioThis page calculates what would happen if the Ivy Mike (USA, 1952) detonated over Shanghai (China). First hydrogen bomb test.China's largest city by population and one of the world's busiest container ports. With an urban-core density of about 3,900 people per km², even a relatively small detonation over the city center would affect a large population.The Ivy Mike delivers 10.4 Mt of explosive yield — 693× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb. The 5 PSI moderate-blast zone — where most residential buildings collapse — would extend to 21.80 km. Light blast damage and shattered windows would reach 62.01 km. Given Shanghai's urban density (~3,900/km²), this scenario yields an estimated 3,523,451 immediate fatalities and about 2,064,787 additional injured.How many people would die in Shanghai from a Ivy Mike strike?An air burst of the Ivy Mike over Shanghai could cause an estimated 3,523,451 immediate fatalities and 2,064,787 additional injuries. The fireball alone (radius 5.86 km) would kill approximately 421,286 people; the severe-blast zone (20 PSI, radius 10.20 km) would add 828,330; the moderate-blast zone (5 PSI, radius 21.80 km) would add 2,273,835 more. Real numbers depend heavily on time of day, sheltering, weather, and altitude of detonation.What is the blast radius of the Ivy Mike on Shanghai?For an air burst over Shanghai: fireball 5.86 km, severe blast (20 PSI) 10.20 km, moderate blast (5 PSI) 21.80 km, light blast (1 PSI) 62.01 km. Thermal radiation causes 3rd-degree burns out to 29.72 km. A surface burst would shrink the blast radii by roughly 40 percent but generate massive radioactive fallout extending ~485 km from ground zero.Is the Ivy Mike bigger than the bomb that hit Hiroshima?The Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy) had a yield of about 15 kilotons. The Ivy Mike at 10.4 Mt is 693× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb.Casualty math uses Shanghai's urban-core density and the scaling laws on the methodology page.
