About this scenarioThis page calculates what would happen if the Castle Bravo (USA, 1954) detonated over Sydney (Australia). Most powerful US nuclear test.The largest city in Australia and host of the 2000 Summer Olympics. With an urban-core density of about 2,100 people per km², even a relatively small detonation over the city center would affect a large population.The Castle Bravo delivers 15 Mt of explosive yield — 1,000× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb. The 5 PSI moderate-blast zone — where most residential buildings collapse — would extend to 24.60 km. Light blast damage and shattered windows would reach 69.98 km. Given Sydney's urban density (~2,100/km²), this scenario yields an estimated 2,416,023 immediate fatalities and about 1,415,820 additional injured.How many people would die in Sydney from a Castle Bravo strike?An air burst of the Castle Bravo over Sydney could cause an estimated 2,416,023 immediate fatalities and 1,415,820 additional injuries. The fireball alone (radius 6.79 km) would kill approximately 304,073 people; the severe-blast zone (20 PSI, radius 11.51 km) would add 552,786; the moderate-blast zone (5 PSI, radius 24.60 km) would add 1,559,164 more. Real numbers depend heavily on time of day, sheltering, weather, and altitude of detonation.What is the blast radius of the Castle Bravo on Sydney?For an air burst over Sydney: fireball 6.79 km, severe blast (20 PSI) 11.51 km, moderate blast (5 PSI) 24.60 km, light blast (1 PSI) 69.98 km. Thermal radiation causes 3rd-degree burns out to 34.54 km. A surface burst would shrink the blast radii by roughly 40 percent but generate massive radioactive fallout extending ~562 km from ground zero.Is the Castle Bravo bigger than the bomb that hit Hiroshima?The Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy) had a yield of about 15 kilotons. The Castle Bravo at 15 Mt is 1,000× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb.Casualty math uses Sydney's urban-core density and the scaling laws on the methodology page.
