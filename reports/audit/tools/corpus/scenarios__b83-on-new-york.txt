About this scenarioThis page calculates what would happen if the B83 (USA, 1983) detonated over New York (USA). Most powerful US weapon in active service.The most populous city in the United States and a global financial capital, with a metropolitan area approaching 20 million people. With an urban-core density of about 10,000 people per km², even a relatively small detonation over the city center would affect a large population.The B83 delivers 1.2 Mt of explosive yield — 80× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb. The 5 PSI moderate-blast zone — where most residential buildings collapse — would extend to 10.69 km. Light blast damage and shattered windows would reach 30.41 km. Given New York's urban density (~10,000/km²), this scenario yields an estimated 2,172,308 immediate fatalities and about 1,272,999 additional injured.How many people would die in New York from a B83 strike?An air burst of the B83 over New York could cause an estimated 2,172,308 immediate fatalities and 1,272,999 additional injuries. The fireball alone (radius 2.47 km) would kill approximately 191,969 people; the severe-blast zone (20 PSI, radius 5.00 km) would add 578,455; the moderate-blast zone (5 PSI, radius 10.69 km) would add 1,401,884 more. Real numbers depend heavily on time of day, sheltering, weather, and altitude of detonation.What is the blast radius of the B83 on New York?For an air burst over New York: fireball 2.47 km, severe blast (20 PSI) 5.00 km, moderate blast (5 PSI) 10.69 km, light blast (1 PSI) 30.41 km. Thermal radiation causes 3rd-degree burns out to 12.26 km. A surface burst would shrink the blast radii by roughly 40 percent but generate massive radioactive fallout extending ~205 km from ground zero.Is the B83 bigger than the bomb that hit Hiroshima?The Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy) had a yield of about 15 kilotons. The B83 at 1.2 Mt is 80× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb.Casualty math uses New York's urban-core density and the scaling laws on the methodology page.
