About this scenarioThis page calculates what would happen if the Fat Man (USA, 1945) detonated over Moscow (Russia). Nagasaki bomb.The capital of Russia and the largest city in Europe by population. With an urban-core density of about 4,900 people per km², even a relatively small detonation over the city center would affect a large population.The Fat Man delivers 21 kt of explosive yield — comparable in yield to the Hiroshima bomb. The 5 PSI moderate-blast zone — where most residential buildings collapse — would extend to 2.81 km. Light blast damage and shattered windows would reach 8.00 km. Given Moscow's urban density (~4,900/km²), this scenario yields an estimated 73,709 immediate fatalities and about 43,194 additional injured.How many people would die in Moscow from a Fat Man strike?An air burst of the Fat Man over Moscow could cause an estimated 73,709 immediate fatalities and 43,194 additional injuries. The fireball alone (radius 0.49 km) would kill approximately 3,697 people; the severe-blast zone (20 PSI, radius 1.32 km) would add 22,445; the moderate-blast zone (5 PSI, radius 2.81 km) would add 47,567 more. Real numbers depend heavily on time of day, sheltering, weather, and altitude of detonation.What is the blast radius of the Fat Man on Moscow?For an air burst over Moscow: fireball 0.49 km, severe blast (20 PSI) 1.32 km, moderate blast (5 PSI) 2.81 km, light blast (1 PSI) 8.00 km. Thermal radiation causes 3rd-degree burns out to 2.33 km. A surface burst would shrink the blast radii by roughly 40 percent but generate massive radioactive fallout extending ~41 km from ground zero.Is the Fat Man bigger than the bomb that hit Hiroshima?The Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy) had a yield of about 15 kilotons. The Fat Man at 21 kt is comparable in yield to the Hiroshima bomb.Casualty math uses Moscow's urban-core density and the scaling laws on the methodology page.
