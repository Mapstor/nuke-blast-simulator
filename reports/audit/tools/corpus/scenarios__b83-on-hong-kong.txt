About this scenarioThis page calculates what would happen if the B83 (USA, 1983) detonated over Hong Kong (China). Most powerful US weapon in active service.A special administrative region of China and one of the world's most densely populated territories. With an urban-core density of about 7,100 people per km², even a relatively small detonation over the city center would affect a large population.The B83 delivers 1.2 Mt of explosive yield — 80× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb. The 5 PSI moderate-blast zone — where most residential buildings collapse — would extend to 10.69 km. Light blast damage and shattered windows would reach 30.41 km. Given Hong Kong's urban density (~7,100/km²), this scenario yields an estimated 1,542,338 immediate fatalities and about 903,830 additional injured.How many people would die in Hong Kong from a B83 strike?An air burst of the B83 over Hong Kong could cause an estimated 1,542,338 immediate fatalities and 903,830 additional injuries. The fireball alone (radius 2.47 km) would kill approximately 136,298 people; the severe-blast zone (20 PSI, radius 5.00 km) would add 410,703; the moderate-blast zone (5 PSI, radius 10.69 km) would add 995,337 more. Real numbers depend heavily on time of day, sheltering, weather, and altitude of detonation.What is the blast radius of the B83 on Hong Kong?For an air burst over Hong Kong: fireball 2.47 km, severe blast (20 PSI) 5.00 km, moderate blast (5 PSI) 10.69 km, light blast (1 PSI) 30.41 km. Thermal radiation causes 3rd-degree burns out to 12.26 km. A surface burst would shrink the blast radii by roughly 40 percent but generate massive radioactive fallout extending ~205 km from ground zero.Is the B83 bigger than the bomb that hit Hiroshima?The Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy) had a yield of about 15 kilotons. The B83 at 1.2 Mt is 80× more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb.Casualty math uses Hong Kong's urban-core density and the scaling laws on the methodology page.
