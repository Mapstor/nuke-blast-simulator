Does Iran have nuclear weapons?
Every major open assessment places Iran outside the group of nuclear-armed states. It is a party to the Non-Proliferation Treaty and has long enriched uranium under IAEA safeguards, but it is not assessed to possess a weapon.
FAS and SIPRI both place Iran outside their lists of the world’s nine nuclear-armed states. [  1 ,    6  ]  The US intelligence community’s 2025 public assessment, as quoted by the Congressional Research Service, was that “Iran is not building a nuclear weapon,” [  7  ]  though CRS describes the current status of the program as unclear. [  7  ]

Uranium enriched to  20% or more  U-235 is called  highly enriched uranium (HEU) . [  4  ]  Weapons-grade uranium is usually described as about  90%  U-235, while power reactors use 3–5%. [  5  ]  Iran’s 60% material is therefore highly enriched and well above reactor grade, but below the level typically used in weapons.
What the IAEA can and can’t verify now
The strikes and Iran’s 2025 cooperation-suspension law left the IAEA unable to keep full track of the program. For more than a year the Agency has lost continuity of knowledge of Iran’s nuclear material, [  8  ]  and it reports that it can no longer discharge its Non-Proliferation-Treaty safeguards responsibilities in Iran [  8  ]  — a situation its Director General calls a serious proliferation concern. ================ RUSSIA INTRO ================
Russia holds the world's largest nuclear arsenal, an estimated 5,420 warheads — about 44% of the world's roughly 12,187 warheads and more than any other country. It and the United States (about 5,042) together account for most of the warheads on Earth.

================ US INTRO ================
The United States has the world's second-largest nuclear arsenal, an estimated 5,042 warheads (about 41% of the world's roughly 12,187 warheads), and remains the only country ever to have used a nuclear weapon in war. Only Russia, with about 5,420, holds more.
